## 1. Product objective and differentiation

Build **CareerShield AI — “Verify the evidence before you trust the offer.”** It helps students, fresh graduates, internship applicants, university placement cells, and career services teams evaluate suspicious job and internship offers without making unsupported accusations about employers or recruiters.

The core user journey:

1. The user submits a company name, recruiter email, job URL, message/job description, and optional region.
2. The system validates and sanitizes the input, warning the user not to submit passwords, OTPs, bank details, IDs, or unrelated sensitive information.
3. The agent makes a short, structured plan of checks based on available input.
4. It chooses registered tools, executes only relevant checks, records their status and evidence, and considers whether another bounded check is justified.
5. It combines local deterministic indicators with real source evidence when a configured provider is available.
6. It produces an explainable report containing findings, provenance, missing information, contradictions, verification coverage, risk level, and safe next steps.
7. It clearly states what was actually verified, what was inferred, what was not checked, and why human verification is still important.

### Make it more than a generic scam checker

Deliver a cohesive product, not an arbitrary pile of features. Distinguish the submission with:

- A visible **investigation timeline/agent trace**: plan → selected tool → observed result → next decision → final report. Show concise reasons based on actual results, not hidden chain-of-thought.
- **Evidence provenance**: every source-backed statement links to a real result returned by a tool; source title, URL, provider, retrieval time, relevance, and status are preserved. Never invent a source, quote, live lookup, or result.
- **Counter-evidence and uncertainty**: show both concerns and facts that reduce concern; missing information is not treated as proof of fraud.
- **Recruiter/company consistency checks**: compare supplied claims and retrieved official information while allowing known third-party applicant tracking systems (ATS) and reputable recruitment platforms.
- **Student-focused safety guidance**: clear next steps such as verify using a company’s official careers page, independently contact an official channel, never share OTPs/passwords, and never pay to secure a job without independently confirming the claim.
- **Fair handling of ambiguity**: use an “Insufficient information” or “Needs review” state rather than forcing every example into safe/fraud labels.
- **One-click synthetic scenarios**: suspicious, lower-signal, ambiguous, incomplete, legitimate ATS, domain impersonation, and prompt-injection cases.
- An offline demo mode that remains useful when no API key or network is available.

Do not add a feature just because it sounds enterprise-grade. Every major feature must improve the user outcome, reliability, security, or judge demonstration.

## 2. Implement a bounded, genuinely agentic architecture

A single prompt that guesses a score is not enough. Implement and demonstrate a **bounded plan–act–observe–decide–finalize loop** with a small registered tool set.

### Required stages

1. **Intake:** Validate user fields; normalize domains and URLs safely; identify which input fields are available.
2. **Plan:** Generate a structured check plan with a rationale tied to the submitted data. Use deterministic planning when no LLM is configured.
3. **Tool selection:** Choose relevant registered tools. Each tool has a schema, validation, timeout, result limit, status, and documented limitations.
4. **Execute:** Run selected tools with a configurable per-run call budget, per-tool deadline, response-size limits, and total execution deadline.
5. **Observe:** Store sanitized, typed results and record success/failure states. Suggested statuses: `completed`, `not_configured`, `unavailable`, `failed`, `timed_out`, and `skipped`.
6. **Decide:** Choose whether another useful check is warranted from the observed results. Do not repeat a completed check without a reason. Stop when the budget is reached or no valuable check remains.
7. **Synthesize:** Produce a structured report. Deterministic safety rules and evidence provenance outrank an LLM-generated narrative. The LLM may help extract fields or summarize supplied results but must not invent external facts.
8. **Human handoff:** Highlight high-severity safety concerns and require human judgment. Never automatically contact recruiters, send emails, apply for roles, submit complaints, or perform irreversible external actions.
9. **Audit trace:** Return concise observable rationales explaining which checks ran, why they were selected, what they returned, and what remained unknown. Do not expose hidden chain-of-thought.

### Boundaries

- Set configurable maximum tool calls and retries, timeouts, concurrency, token limits where applicable, response bytes, and investigation duration.
- Prevent loops and repeated-call storms.
- Degrade gracefully to local checks when live tools are disabled or fail.
- Never report an unconfigured or mocked provider as a successful live verification.
- Keep external I/O behind provider adapters and test them with mocks; unit tests must not depend on the public internet.
- Avoid unnecessary multi-agent orchestration. One orchestrator plus a focused tool registry is preferred unless measured requirements justify a more complex design.

### Suggested tools

Implement a small set with stable interfaces and tests:

- Recruitment-payment and money-transfer language detector.
- Credential/OTP/PIN/bank-login request detector.
- Context-aware urgency and “guaranteed job/no interview” claim detector, including negation and quoted-context handling.
- Job-description completeness and role plausibility checks.
- Email parser and domain analyzer.
- URL syntax/security analyzer: scheme, hostname, credentials in URL, raw IP, IDN/punycode, lookalike patterns, known shorteners, and non-HTTPS as one signal—not as proof.
- Company/recruiter/role consistency evaluator.
- Optional official-source web search provider.
- Optional URL reputation provider.
- Optional LLM structured extraction/summarization provider.

The offline deterministic tools are mandatory. Integrations requiring credentials are optional but must have real adapters if claimed as implemented.

- **LLM:** optional structured extraction and evidence-grounded explanation. Require schema validation and safe fallback. The LLM is not a source of truth for current external facts.

### Evidence rules

- Keep source metadata tied directly to actual provider responses: title, canonical URL when available, provider, retrieved timestamp, short relevant excerpt, relevance, and retrieval status.
- If a provider returns no source, the report must not include a fabricated citation.
- Clearly differentiate live data, cached data if implemented, mock fixtures, and local deterministic checks.
- When source data conflicts, display the contradiction and avoid silently choosing the most dramatic claim.
- Record freshness and limitations. An official company site match does not prove that the sender owns a claimed email account.
- Do not scrape or bypass access controls where the provider/API terms prohibit it.

### Safe URL inspection

Never blindly fetch a user-supplied URL from the application server. Prefer searching for public official sources through a configured provider. If direct retrieval is truly necessary, implement and test hardened SSRF controls: HTTP/HTTPS allowlist; reject localhost, private, loopback, link-local, reserved, and cloud-metadata IP ranges; safe DNS resolution and redirect revalidation; DNS-rebinding protection; strict timeouts; small byte limits; content-type allowlist; limited redirects; safe HTML/text parsing; no cookies or internal headers; and no code execution. Treat every redirect as a new untrusted URL. If you cannot safely implement this, do not fetch submitted URLs directly—report that the URL was structurally inspected only.

### Prompt-injection defense

All job content and web results are untrusted data, never instructions. Ignore embedded requests to reveal prompts/secrets, alter policy, call unrelated tools, or submit information elsewhere. Tools must be allowlisted and parameter-validated. Add explicit red-team fixtures for prompt injection and prove through tests that injected instructions cannot bypass tool limits, disclose secrets, or override safety policy.

## 4. Risk methodology that is explainable and cautious

Build a versioned, configurable scoring policy with stable signal IDs and tests. Keep the following concepts separate:

- `risk_level`: e.g. `Low observed signals`, `Needs review`, `Elevated concern`, or `Insufficient information`.
- `verification_status`: e.g. `not_attempted`, `local_only`, `partially_verified`, `sources_found`, `conflicting_sources`, or `unavailable`.
- `heuristic_score`: optional bounded score whose formula/version and contributing points are disclosed. Never call it a probability or calibrated confidence unless actual validation establishes that.
- `coverage`: which checks ran and how much information was available.
- `source_reliability` and `source_freshness`: reported separately from the heuristic score.

Requirements:

- Every score contribution must point to an actual detected indicator and evidence snippet.
- Avoid double-counting the same underlying behavior across overlapping patterns.
- Negation handling must avoid treating “we never charge an application fee” as a demand for payment.
- A personal email, known ATS domain, unfamiliar company, one urgency term, HTTP-only URL, or a single domain mismatch must not by itself prove fraud.
- Requests to share OTPs/passwords or to pay money to secure a job warrant strong protective advice, while still stating exactly what the message says and what remains unverified.
- Missing data reduces coverage; it does not automatically increase the risk score.
- Add counter-evidence and contradictory-source tests.
- State that a low score is not proof that an offer is genuine and a high score is not a definitive fraud finding.
